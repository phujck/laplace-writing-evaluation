A learner with finite representational resources must decide not only how to change its model, but which changes deserve authority and when additional capacity is worth buying. We develop a finite framework in which the evaluator is part of the state being regulated. A shared quadratic register supplies a common cost ledger and an exact boundary between reuse, arbitration and partition purchase. Relative-entropy regularisation then gives a normalised selector over the actions actually available, with gains evaluated at the states those actions execute. An explicit model/evaluator controller converges under stated damping and coupling conditions, without assuming that convergence establishes adaptive value. We derive weighted free-energy contraction and show that OR aggregation of externally frozen independent update opportunities closes exactly in cumulative hazard. A smaller common-intercept representation has a calculable event-probability error, while its threshold response equals conditional event multiplicity. The same distinction between marginalisation and constrained closure leads to a quantum recovery criterion. A sequence of figures connects the calculations to a matched synthetic policy comparison, including its adverse predictive result. The proposed laws of learning are thus operational requirements on an embodied model-revision scheme: declare its evaluator, price its executed actions, and test what survives a change of representation.
